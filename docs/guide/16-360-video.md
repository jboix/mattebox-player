# 16 360° video

`@mattebox/player-360` shows a 360° video from inside. The picture is drawn
onto the inside of a sphere around the viewer, and the viewer looks around
it. It needs WebGL, which every browser down to Chromium 76 has.

## The element

```html
<mattebox-player src="https://example.com/vod/360/master.m3u8" controls="custom">
  <mbx-360></mbx-360>
  <mbx-control-bar>
    <mbx-play-button></mbx-play-button>
    <mbx-seek-bar></mbx-seek-bar>
  </mbx-control-bar>
</mattebox-player>

<script type="module">
  import '@mattebox/player';
  import '@mattebox/player-360';
</script>
```

Place `<mbx-360>` first, right after the video. It covers the whole player,
and the controls placed after it stay on top.

Over Safari's own HLS the video shows flat. So while `<mbx-360>` is inside
a player, the player's default for `native-hls` is `off`: Safari plays HLS
through the engine. Set `native-hls="on"` to give Safari its own HLS back,
for a player whose sources are all flat.

| The viewer            | What happens                                                  |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Drags the picture     | The view turns. The picture follows the pointer               |
| Taps without a drag   | The video plays or pauses, as a click on a flat video does    |
| Turns the wheel       | The view zooms in or out, from 30° to 100° tall               |
| Leans the right stick | A game controller turns the view, faster the further it leans |
| Turns a phone         | The view turns with the device. See [Motion](#motion)         |

While the view shows, the video keeps a 16:9 box. A 360° picture is 2:1,
and the view is a window into it.

The poster shows as on a flat video: until the programme plays or seeks.
Before that the sphere stays hidden. On a new source, the last programme's
picture goes as soon as the video empties.

## How the player knows

The player carries `projection` while its source is 360°. It comes from the
first of these that says so.

1. The `projection` attribute on `<mbx-360>`. It wins over everything.
   `none` keeps a 360° source flat.
2. The source's `projection`, which your page or your connector sets. Use it
   when your content API knows: the stream often does not say.
3. The engine: HLS `REQ-VIDEO-LAYOUT`, and from its release after 0.18.0
   the DASH projection descriptor and the boxes of an MP4 init segment
   (engine guide, chapter 03). The player reads the engine's `projection`
   either way.

```js
player.sources = [{ url: 'https://example.com/360/master.m3u8', projection: 'equirectangular' }];
```

| `projection`           | The picture                                             |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| `equirectangular`      | The whole sphere, 2:1                                   |
| `half-equirectangular` | The half in front of the viewer, 1:1. The back is black |

## A connector

SRG SSR's content API marks a 360° resource with `presentation:
'VIDEO_360'`, and its streams carry no projection of their own. The demo's
connector turns the one into the other:

```js
const sphere = resource.presentation === 'VIDEO_360' ? { projection: 'equirectangular' } : {};
return { url, type: resource.mimeType, ...sphere };
```

## Motion

On a phone or a tablet, the view turns with the device. It is on by
default, because looking around with the device is what a viewer expects of
a 360° video on a phone.

When the system asks for reduced motion (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`),
motion starts off. A view that sways with the hand can make a viewer with a
vestibular disorder ill, and neither the browser nor the system stops the
motion events for them. The element follows the setting when it changes
while the page is open.

| `motion` | Motion starts                                      |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| absent   | On, or off when the system asks for reduced motion |
| `on`     | On, even when the system asks for reduced motion   |
| `off`    | Off                                                |

`motion="on"` overrides a setting the viewer made for their health. Set it
only with `<mbx-motion-button>` in the bar, so the viewer can turn it off.

- The element listens to `deviceorientation` only while the view shows.
- The first event keeps the yaw the view has, so the view does not jump.
  The pitch and the roll follow the device from then on, level with the
  ground.
- A drag, the wheel, the stick and the `yaw` and `pitch` properties still
  work. Their turn adds to the device's, and stays as the device moves.
- A computer without the sensors sends no angles, and nothing changes.
- A new source starts straight ahead again.

iOS 13 and later sends no motion before the page asks, and lets a page ask
only during a touch or a click. The element asks at the viewer's first
touch on the view, once. That touch also plays or pauses, as any tap does.
A refusal turns motion off.

### The motion button

`<mbx-motion-button>` lets the viewer turn the motion on or off, for a
viewer who wants to look around by touch only. Place it in the bar:

```html
<mattebox-player src="https://example.com/vod/360/master.m3u8" controls="custom">
  <mbx-360></mbx-360>
  <mbx-control-bar>
    <mbx-play-button></mbx-play-button>
    <mbx-seek-bar></mbx-seek-bar>
    <mbx-motion-button></mbx-motion-button>
  </mbx-control-bar>
</mattebox-player>
```

- It is a toggle: one name, and `aria-pressed` says whether the motion
  turns the view.
- It is hidden while no 360° view shows, and on a device without motion
  sensors, such as a computer. It shows once the device sends angles, or
  at once on iOS, which sends none before the page asks.
- On iOS its click asks for the permission. A refusal leaves it off.
- The viewer's choice lasts as long as the element. It is not saved: a
  viewer who turned it off to try it would not remember it on the next
  visit. A page that sets `motion` replaces the choice.
- To force one way, set `motion` and leave the button out.

| Name       | Kind      | What it does                              |
| ---------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| `label`    | attribute | The button's name. Turn with the device   |
| `icon-on`  | slot      | The glyph while the motion turns the view |
| `icon-off` | slot      | The glyph while it does not               |

A page with a control of its own calls `setMotion()` on `<mbx-360>` from a
click, reads `motionOn` and `motionAvailable`, and listens to
`motionchange`.

## Headsets

`<mbx-vr-button>` shows the view in a VR headset, through WebXR. Place it in
the bar:

```html
<mattebox-player src="https://example.com/vod/360/master.m3u8" controls="custom">
  <mbx-360></mbx-360>
  <mbx-control-bar>
    <mbx-play-button></mbx-play-button>
    <mbx-seek-bar></mbx-seek-bar>
    <mbx-vr-button></mbx-vr-button>
  </mbx-control-bar>
</mattebox-player>
```

- The button is hidden unless the browser has WebXR, a headset can show an
  `immersive-vr` session, and `<mbx-360>` shows a 360° source. A headset
  plugged in later shows it.
- In the headset the viewer looks around with their head. The view starts
  at the yaw the screen had.
- The picture is the same for both eyes. Stereo pictures (top and bottom,
  side by side) are not split per eye.
- The session ends from the headset's own menu, from the button, or when
  the source becomes flat. The screen view draws again then.
- Without WebXR, which includes every TV, the button stays hidden and
  nothing else changes.

| Name          | Kind      | What it does                                                |
| ------------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| `label-enter` | attribute | The button's name while the screen shows the view. Enter VR |
| `label-exit`  | attribute | The button's name while the headset shows it. Exit VR       |
| `icon-enter`  | slot      | The glyph while the screen shows the view                   |
| `icon-exit`   | slot      | The glyph while the headset shows it                        |

The words come in the 11 languages of [chapter 10](10-languages.md), from
`@mattebox/player-360/locales/<language>`. The ids are `enterVr`,
`exitVr` and `deviceMotion`, in the `Player360Messages` type.

```ts
import { MatteboxPlayerElement } from '@mattebox/player';
import words from '@mattebox/player/locales/de';
import words360 from '@mattebox/player-360/locales/de';

MatteboxPlayerElement.addLocale('de', words, words360);
```

A page with a control of its own calls `enterVR()` on `<mbx-360>` from a
click. The browser starts a session only during a click or a key press.

## When the view does not show

The flat video plays instead, and `unavailable` on `<mbx-360>` says why.

| `unavailable` | Why                                                                                       |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `drm`         | The source is encrypted. A browser does not let a page draw a DRM picture: no player can  |
| `source`      | Another site's video, served without CORS. Set `crossorigin` on the player and allow CORS |
| `webgl`       | The browser gives no WebGL                                                                |

## Attributes and properties

| Name              | Kind      | What it does                                                                              |
| ----------------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `projection`      | attribute | Forces a projection, or `none`. See [How the player knows](#how-the-player-knows)         |
| `fov`             | attribute | The field of view a source starts with, in degrees, 75 by default                         |
| `motion`          | attribute | `on` or `off`: whether the device's motion starts turning the view. See [Motion](#motion) |
| `active`          | attribute | Set by the element while the view shows                                                   |
| `vr`              | attribute | Set by the element while a headset shows the view                                         |
| `unavailable`     | attribute | Set by the element. See [When the view does not show](#when-the-view-does-not-show)       |
| `yaw`             | property  | Degrees the view is turned right of the picture's centre                                  |
| `pitch`           | property  | Degrees the view looks up                                                                 |
| `fov`             | property  | The vertical field of view in degrees, from 30 to 100                                     |
| `immersive`       | property  | True while a headset shows the view                                                       |
| `enterVR()`       | method    | Shows the view in a headset. Call it from a click. See [Headsets](#headsets)              |
| `exitVR()`        | method    | Ends the headset session                                                                  |
| `motionOn`        | property  | True while the device's motion turns the view, as the viewer or `motion` set it           |
| `motionAvailable` | property  | True on a device with motion sensors                                                      |
| `setMotion(on)`   | method    | The viewer's choice, until the page sets `motion`. Call it from a click on iOS            |
| `motionchange`    | event     | `motionOn` or `motionAvailable` changed. It bubbles to the player                         |

A new source starts straight ahead, at `fov`.

## Subtitles

The browser draws subtitles inside the video, under the view. The element
writes the active cues of the showing track over the view instead, without
their styling.

## Parts

| Part        | What it is                          |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------- |
| `view`      | The canvas the sphere is drawn on   |
| `subtitles` | The subtitles written over the view |

## Not yet

- Turning the view with the keyboard. The arrows move focus between the
  controls, so the view needs keys of its own. Set `yaw` and `pitch` from
  your own keys meanwhile.
- Stereo pictures, one half per eye, in a headset.
